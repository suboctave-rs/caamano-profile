María
Caamaño-Alegre

Associate Professor in the Department of Philosophy at the Universidad de Valladolid, working on the incommensurability of scientific theories, theory evaluation, experimental validity, and the methodological peculiarities of the social sciences. Her current interests include data modeling, reliability, underdetermination, and opacities in experimental and simulation contexts.

About

Background

She earned her Ph.D. in Philosophy from the University of Santiago de Compostela (2004) defending a thesis on the problem of the incommensurability of scientific theories, and was a Fulbright postdoctoral fellow at Stanford University before joining the University of Valladolid (2007), where she is now Associate Professor.

Her work sits at the junction of philosophy and methodology of science, philosophy of language, and epistemology — with sustained attention to how theories are compared, how evidence is evaluated, and where experimental validity breaks down, in both the natural and social sciences. She has published in Erkenntnis, Synthese, International Studies in the Philosophy of Science, Foundations of Science, Journal for General Philosophy of Science, and Philosophy Compass, among others. She is an editor of Theoria: An International Journal for Theory, History, and Foundations of Science.

Current project

Data Modeling and the Problem of Ambiguity in Experimental Results

ModAmbEx · PID2024-161297NB-I00 · funded by MICIU/AEI/10.13039/501100011033 and “ERDF A way of making Europe,” European Union

The project addresses the problem of ambiguity in experimental results, with particular attention to empirical ambiguity — the different ways the data from an experiment can be understood or interpreted — and how data modeling shapes the extent of that ambiguity. It examines both traditional experimental cases and emerging empirical research based on computer simulations, focusing on the forms of opacity present in experimental contexts: how uncertainties linked to environment, instrumentation, and error affect the modeling of experimental data, the evidence built from it, and ultimately the theoretical inferences that evidence supports.

Where traditional approaches have privileged theoretical ambiguity — the possibility of applying different theoretical constructs to interpret data — this project turns to the comparatively neglected question of whether the same experimental data can support different empirical phenomena. Theoretical hypotheses, after all, are never confronted with raw data directly, but with phenomena already established through modeling. A distinctive element of the proposal is its emphasis on scaffolds as a conceptual bridge between experimental data and theoretical hypotheses — highlighting the role intermediate assumptions play in building evidence.

Public Engagement

Knowledge Transfer & Media

She is co-director of the annual Valladolid Piensa (Valladolid Thinks) Philosophy Festival since its inception in 2017 (also on YouTube). Each edition opens on World Philosophy Day with three days of activities at landmark locations across the city, creating spaces for reflection that foster interdisciplinary dialogue. The festival is supported by the Valladolid City Council, the Office of the Vice Rector for Communication, Culture, and Sports, and the Faculty of Philosophy and Letters at the Universidad de Valladolid.
